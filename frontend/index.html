How Fire Behaves in Zero Gravity & Why It Matters for Deep Space

On Earth (1g), hot combustion gases expand, become less dense, and rise due to buoyancy, drawing fresh oxygen into the base of a teardrop flame. In microgravity (0g / 10⁻⁶g), buoyancy-driven convection vanishes. Flames become spherical, diffusion-controlled, cooler, and capable of burning at lower oxygen concentrations—posing critical fire safety challenges for ISS, Lunar Gateway, and Mars transit habitats.

— 01 EXOSPHERE / DIFFUSION

Absence of Buoyant Convection

Without gravity pulling denser cold air downward, hot exhaust gases do not rise. Molecular diffusion becomes the primary transport mechanism carrying oxygen inward and combustion products outward, creating symmetric spherical flamelets.

STAGNANT REGIME
+
— 02 VENTILATION / FORCED FLOW

Spacecraft Cabin Ventilation Hazard

Astronauts rely on Environmental Control and Life Support System (ECLSS) fans to prevent CO₂ pockets. However, low-velocity cabin airflow (5–30 cm/s) acts as forced convection, feeding oxygen to smoldering wires and accelerating flame spread.

FORCED CONVECTION

— 03 CHEMICAL KINETICS / LOW-T

Two-Stage "Cool Flames"

Discovered aboard the ISS during NASA's FLEX experiments, hydrocarbon droplets can extinguish visibly yet continue burning invisibly at ~500–800 K via low-temperature peroxide chemistry, requiring specialized optical/ion sensors.

    _THEORETICAL FLUID MECHANICS

    Comparative Analysis: Terrestrial (1g) vs. Microgravity (0g) Combustion Theory

    Theoretical fluid mechanics, dimensionless scaling numbers, and transport equations governing flame structure under gravitational vs. forced-ventilation regimes.

    +

    1. Grashof & Froude Number Scaling

    Gr = (g · β · ΔT · L³) / ν²   |   Fr = U² / (g · L)

    In terrestrial flames, the Grashof number (Gr ~ 10⁴–10⁶) dominates, generating buoyant velocities Ub ≈ √(g·L·ΔT/T∞) ~ 0.5–1.5 m/s. On the ISS (g ≈ 10⁻⁶g₀), Gr → 0, collapsing natural convection. When cabin ventilation Uair is introduced, the mixed-convection ratio Gr / Re² determines whether forced airflow or diffusion governs flame geometry.

    +

    2. Damköhler Number & Radiative Extinction

    Da = τflow / τchem = (L / Udiff) / τchem

    Because molecular diffusion is slow, characteristic residence times τflow increase dramatically in microgravity. While high residence time initially boosts soot nucleation, expanded spherical flame surface area increases volumetric radiative heat loss (Q̇rad ∝ T⁴·Vflame). At large droplet diameters, radiative loss drops temperature below the hot-flame threshold, triggering extinction or transition into a low-temperature Cool Flame.

    +

    3. Lewis Number & Diffusive-Thermal Instability

    Le = α / Dmass = k / (ρ · cp · Dmass)

    In lean hydrogen or methane mixtures (Le < 1), mass diffusivity of the deficient reactant exceeds thermal diffusivity. In zero-g, this triggers cellular flame structures and isolated flame balls (studied in NASA SOFBALL) that remain stable far below terrestrial lean flammability limits.
